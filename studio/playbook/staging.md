# Staging: one hero at a time, a set that keeps filling

The rules below apply to every mode; `modes/<mode>/GUIDE.md` adds that mode's own numbers on top.

## Building a shot

Picture each shot as a stage that starts busy and gets busier.

- **Open in motion.** Frame zero of the reel already has something happening in it: no blank set, no title card.
- **Each word the voice leans on adds a piece or changes one.** Pieces that came before shuffle aside or get smaller; they don't leave, and they keep breathing a little.
- **Around 3 s in, the stage holds three to six pieces.** That is full, not crowded, because only one of them is loud.
- **The hero is whatever arrived last.** Nothing else changes the frame as much, nothing else wears the accent colour, the host aims there and the lens drifts its way. The rest stay settled, smaller and plain.
- **The stage leaves as one.** At the cut the whole set is swallowed by the next object, drops away or whips out of frame together; never piece by piece.

## Rhythm

| Event | Rate |
|---|---|
| A piece arrives or changes | on each leaned-on word, roughly every 0.4–0.8 s |
| A big beat (slam, cut, whip, wipe, topple, host move) | 0.7 or more per second |
| A small movement somewhere | every 0.3–0.5 s |
| A frame with nothing moving | 1.2 s at the very most |
| A state the viewer has to read | stays put for 0.4 s or longer |

A big beat hits **on** the word that carries the sentence (usually its noun or its number), never early. A pause in the voice is not a pause in the picture.

**If that word is the last one in the shot,** the beat still lands on it, but it has to finish before the transition begins (each mode guide gives its transition length, such as the 0.3 s print sweep). Push the cut later before you pull the beat earlier; if the cut is fixed, use the word before. A beat hidden under a transition is a wasted beat.

**Let the payoff sit.** A shot's payoff (the reveal, the landed number, the stamp, the end state) stays readable for **1.0 s or more** before the cut. When the line is too short for that, the payoff gets a shot of its own or the cut moves.

## Text on screen

- Anything the viewer is meant to read is 40 px or larger; interface text shown inside a phone or a browser can go down to 28 px. Tiny labels and timestamps are decoration and may be smaller.
- Beyond the captions, keep the must-read text to about six words.
- Rings, boxes and underlines land exactly on what they mark; check them at full size.

## Host and accent

- Where the mode has a host, it aims at the hero nearly all the time, follows it when it moves, and changes gesture or expression with every phrase. It may step to the centre once in a reel; the rest of the time it keeps to its corner so the content stays big.
- The accent colour belongs to the one word or object that matters right now. Accent everything and you have accented nothing.

## Getting from one idea to the next

- Between ideas use a wipe, whip or swoop, picked from the words when possible (a page flip on "tomorrow", a freeze on "stop").
- Within one idea, cut hard.
- Two kinds of transition per reel at most, not counting the CTA.

## Captions

- Word by word with the voice; one emphasised word per phrase (two at the most), the noun or number doing the work.
- They keep to their zone and stay off the hero.

## The platform's chrome

Instagram and TikTok lay their own buttons and text over the bottom ~300 px and the right ~120 px, so anything important sits above y ≈ 1400 and between x 60 and 1020.

## Sound

The voice is in front; effects sit roughly 18 dB below it. One effect per event that deserves it: a swoosh for a transition, a pop when something appears, a tick on a counter, a thud or stamp on a slam. No bells or dings, and no bursts of effects stacked on top of each other. Music is optional; many creators add a track inside the app.

## The last seconds

The ask shows the keyword large (as a pill, a sign or a card) next to something that says "comment": a comment sheet, or the host pointing. Keep the final frame up for about half a second after the voice stops.
